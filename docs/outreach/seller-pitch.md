# The seller pitch

**Status: draft. Nobody sends this. The owner sends it, when the owner sends it.**

Written for the warm case: an artist who is already in the survey (`index.html`), so
the opener is "we already wrote about you," not a cold introduction. A version for
artists outside the 60 would need a different opener — not drafted here, lower priority
until the warm list is exhausted.

Two lengths below: a short DM/email version (lead with this), and the longer version
for anyone who asks "wait, what actually is this" before committing.

---

## Short version (DM / email opener)

> Hey [name] — I run People Hate Jazz, the magazine/playlist thing that put you on the
> [survey/roundup] alongside [one other artist from the piece, for credibility]. Building
> a small marketplace attached to it — one-of-one items from the artists in it, sold at
> auction to the people already reading about them. No fee to list, no card details, no
> company yet: I put you up, people bid, if it sells I connect you with the winner and
> you two settle directly (bank transfer, whatever works). I take nothing until there's
> a reason to. Would you have one thing worth putting up — a test pressing, a page from
> a notebook, a piece of gear, an hour of your time? Happy to send more detail either way.

That's the whole ask in one message. It should be skimmable in ten seconds and answerable
with a one-line reply.

## Longer version (for a reply that asks for detail)

**What this is.** People Hate Jazz started as a written survey of sixty artists, then
grew a marketplace next to it — sixty private shops, one per artist, selling things that
only exist once. Right now the shops carry a fictional catalogue clearly marked as a
prototype; the site is honest about that on every page. The point of reaching out is to
replace exactly one of those fictional lots with a real one from you.

**What it asks of you.** One item and a few sentences about it. That's it. No inventory
commitment, no schedule, no exclusivity. Examples of what's sold this way elsewhere: a
test pressing, a handwritten chart or lyric page, a piece of stage-used gear, a signed
print, a limited tape run, or time — a mix session, a lesson, a phone call about how a
track got made. If nothing physical exists, time is a real lot.

**What you get.** The winning bid, paid to you directly by the winning bidder — nobody's
card details touch this site. You also get an audience that already read something
specific about your work, which is a warmer buyer than a stranger off a marketplace
search.

**What it costs you.** Nothing to list. No cut is taken today, because there is no
payment processor wired up yet and no company to take one through — see below. If this
works and turns into something recurring, a percentage would be the honest way to fund
it, and that conversation happens before any money changes hands the second time, not
buried in fine print now.

**How settlement actually works, today.** There is no card payment on this site yet, on
purpose — that requires a legal entity and a payment processor, and neither exists
before there's proof anyone wants to buy anything. So: a lot closes, I tell you who won
and how much, I introduce you two, and you arrange payment and shipping/handover
directly — bank transfer, PayPal, cash at a show, whatever the two of you are comfortable
with. I'm not in that transaction and don't touch the money. This is temporary
scaffolding, not the plan — the moment there's real bidding volume, that's the signal to
build the part that takes payment properly.

**Why now, and why you.** The site can't prove any of this works with an invented
catalogue forever. You're one of the sixty already covered, so this isn't a cold pitch —
you're already a specific paragraph on the site, not a stranger asking for a favor.

**What I need if you're in.** A photo or two, a couple of sentences about the item
(condition, story, why it exists), a reserve if you want a floor, and a rough sense of how
long you want the auction open. I'll write it up in the house style and send it back for
your okay before anything goes live — nothing of yours goes up without you seeing it first.

## Objection handling (for the owner's reference, not to paste verbatim)

- **"Is this legit / who are you?"** — point at the existing site: the survey is real,
  the writing about them is real and already public, the store's prototype banner is
  honest about what's fictional. The ask is small and reversible.
- **"What if it doesn't sell?"** — nothing lost; pull the lot, no cost was ever incurred.
- **"What's the cut?"** — none right now, and say why (no processor, no entity — see
  pitch). Don't promise a specific future number; that's a M4 decision for later.
- **"Can I see the listing before it's public?"** — yes, always. Say so up front; it
  removes the biggest hesitation for someone protective of their image.
